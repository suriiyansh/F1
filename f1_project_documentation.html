01 — Introduction

Project Overview

This report delves into the captivating realm of Formula 1 Race Intelligence, providing a comprehensive visual exploration of key aspects and trends in the world's premier motorsport competition. Through visual representations, readers can gain valuable insights into driver performance, team championship trajectories, tyre compound strategies, circuit-wise race participation, driver reliability, and DNF analysis, as well as global podium achievements.

These visualizations enable intuitive analysis, allowing stakeholders — including team strategists, race engineers, motorsport journalists, and F1 fans — to uncover patterns, identify areas of strength or concern, and make data-driven decisions.

By harnessing the power of Tableau, this report not only presents the data in a visually appealing manner but also provides an interactive experience for readers to explore the intricacies of Formula 1 race intelligence. The dataset covers 5,419 race entries across 17 seasons (2009–2025) with 30 drivers across 10 constructor teams.

02 — Problem Understanding

Business Problem

Formula 1 generates enormous volumes of race data across every season. Teams, broadcasters, and regulators all need to analyze this data from different perspectives and with different goals. The core challenge is creating a unified intelligence platform that serves multiple analyst roles simultaneously.

Business Requirements

  • Enable team strategists to benchmark performance against rivals using historical points data
  • Allow broadcast analysts to quickly retrieve driver and country-level statistics for live coverage
  • Support technical regulation analysts in identifying DNF patterns and evaluating reliability improvements
  • Provide a responsive and interactive dashboard that supports season-level and driver-level filtering
  • Visualize 17 seasons of data in a single unified Tableau workbook

Social & Business Impact

The F1 Race Intelligence Dashboard enables data-driven decision making at multiple organizational levels. For constructor teams, smarter tyre strategies and recruitment decisions based on talent-pool analytics can directly impact championship outcomes. For broadcasters, rapid access to compelling statistics improves fan engagement. For the FIA, reliability data supports evidence-based technical regulation improvements that reduce driver risk.

Literature Survey

Formula 1 analytics has grown significantly with the adoption of real-time telemetry systems. Academic research by Bekker & Lotriet (2018) demonstrated that predictive models using historical race data can forecast race outcomes with up to 73% accuracy. Tableau has been used in sports analytics across the NFL, NBA, and Premier League for real-time performance monitoring and fan-facing infographics. This project adapts these methodologies for the F1 domain.

03 — Analyst Scenarios

User Personas & Scenarios

Scenario 1

Mr. Arnold — F1 Team Performance Strategist

A senior performance strategist at a mid-tier F1 constructor team. He benchmarks his team's championship points trajectory against Red Bull and Mercedes, evaluates how tyre compound strategies affect race outcomes across different circuit types, and identifies which drivers and nationalities represent the strongest talent pools globally. Using the F1 Race Intelligence Dashboard built on 5,419 race records across 17 seasons, Rajan aims to develop a data-driven race and recruitment strategy to climb the Constructors' Championship standings.

Scenario 2

Ms. Iisa — Motorsport Broadcast Analyst

A motorsport data journalist and broadcast analyst at a leading sports media network preparing pre-race and post-race content for Formula 1 coverage. She needs to quickly retrieve the Driver Performance Leaderboard showing cumulative points across seasons, identify which countries have hosted the most Grand Prix rounds, and visualize podium achievement distributions by driver and team for compelling on-screen infographics. The F1 Race Intelligence Dashboard enables Divya to rapidly pull data-rich visuals and statistics for live broadcast, editorial pieces, and fan engagement content.

Scenario 3

Mr. Gilchrist — F1 Technical Regulation Analyst

Works at the FIA as a technical regulation compliance analyst. He studies DNF (Did Not Finish) patterns across drivers to identify whether specific failure types — engine failures, brake failures, collisions, mechanical issues — are concentrated in particular teams or circuit types. He evaluates driver reliability scores across all 17 seasons to assess whether regulatory changes have improved race completion rates. Using the Driver Reliability Analysis stacked bar chart on the Race Intelligence Dashboard, Arvind identifies systemic failure patterns and presents evidence-based recommendations for technical regulation improvements.

04 — Data Collection & Extraction

Dataset Description

The F1 race dataset was constructed to represent realistic historical Formula 1 race results from 2009 through 2025, covering 17 complete seasons. The dataset contains 5,419 rows with each row representing a single driver's result in a single race.

Data Schema

ColumnTypeDescription
Race_IDIntegerUnique identifier for each race event
SeasonIntegerF1 season year (2009–2025)
RoundIntegerRace round number within the season
Race_NameStringOfficial Grand Prix name (e.g., "Monaco GP")
CircuitStringCircuit name (e.g., "Circuit de Monaco")
CountryStringHost country of the Grand Prix
DriverStringFull driver name
NationalityStringDriver's nationality
TeamStringConstructor team name
PositionStringRace finishing position or "DNF"
Finishing_PositionIntegerNumeric position (999 = DNF)
PointsIntegerChampionship points scored
Laps_CompletedIntegerNumber of laps completed
Tyre_CompoundStringPrimary tyre used (Soft/Medium/Hard/Inter/Wet)
DNFString"Yes" or "No"
DNF_ReasonStringCause of retirement if DNF
Fastest_LapString"Yes" or "No" (bonus point 2019+)

Data Inspection & Validation

  • No null values in required fields (Season, Driver, Team, Points)
  • DNF_Reason is empty string (not null) for finishers — handled as blank in Tableau
  • Finishing_Position = 999 is used as a sentinel for DNF — filter this out in position-based views
  • Points correctly reflect F1 scoring: 25-18-15-12-10-8-6-4-2-1 with +1 for fastest lap (2019+)
  • Season filter in Tableau should use discrete (blue pill) not continuous for correct axis labels
